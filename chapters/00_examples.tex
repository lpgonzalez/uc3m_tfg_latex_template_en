\section{Simple lists}

Lists are easy. To create them:
\begin{itemize}
  \item List entries start with the \verb|\item| command.
  \item In this kind of list every entry starts with a bullet.
  \item The length of each entry is arbitrary.
  \item And so is the number of items.
\end{itemize}

% -------------------------

\section{Numbered lists}

Numbered lists are easy too:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Items are numbered automatically.
  \item Numbers are generated automatically by the \texttt{enumerate} environment.
  \item Another entry.
\end{enumerate}

% -------------------------

\subsection{Nested numbered lists}

Example of nested numbered lists:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item First level.
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item Second level.
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item Third level.
      \item Third level.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Second level.
  \end{enumerate}
  \item First level.
\end{enumerate}

% -------------------------

\section{Custom lists}

Both bulleted and numbered lists let you change the leading marker with
\verb|\item[marker]|:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item First point.
  \item Second point.
  \item[!] Third point, starting with an exclamation mark.
  \item[$\blacksquare$] Now starting with a black square.
  \item[NOTE] This entry has no bullet.
  \item[] Blank entry?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Line wrapping with tabularx}

% The tabularx package (loaded in tfg_uc3m.sty) fixes the total table width
% (here \textwidth) and shares the space among the X columns, whose content
% wraps automatically (word-wrap). Ideal for long text.
\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{|c|l|X|}
    \hline
    \textbf{ID} & \textbf{Requirement} & \textbf{Description} \\ \hline
    FR-01 & Sign-up & The system shall let users register with an email and a password, validating the email format and uniqueness. \\ \hline
    FR-02 & Authentication & The system shall let users log in and shall keep the session securely active for a configurable time. \\ \hline
  \end{tabularx}
  \caption{A \texttt{tabularx} table: it spans the text width and cells wrap}
  \label{tab:tabularx}
\end{table}

\subsection{Rescaled to fit (\texttt{\textbackslash resizebox})}

% \resizebox (from the graphicx package) scales the WHOLE table uniformly to the
% given width (here \textwidth). Handy for wide, many-column tables: the type
% looks smaller but uniform, and the table fits within the margins.

      Expense &  80 &  90 &  95 &  85 & 100 & 105 & 110 & 108 & 102 & 112 & 115 & 120 \\
      Balance &  20 &  30 &  35 &  25 &  40 &  45 &  50 &  47 &  43 &  53 &  55 &  60 \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}}
  \caption{A table rescaled with \texttt{\textbackslash resizebox} to fit the text width}

\subsection{Sideways table (rotated content)}

% For a wide table you would rather NOT rescale, you can rotate its content 90°
% with \rotatebox (from the graphicx package) inside an [H] float: it then
% appears right here, after the other tables, without "floating" to another
% page. The caption stays horizontal. The tick mark \tfgcheckmark (defined in
% tfg_uc3m.sty) is handy in comparison tables. If you want to rotate the whole
% PAGE instead, see the next section.
\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \rotatebox{90}{%
    \begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|c|c|}
      \hline
      Criterion   & Option A & Option B & Option C & Option D & Option E \\ \hline
      Cost        & Low   & Medium & High  & Medium & Low   \\
      Performance & Medium & High  & High  & Low   & High  \\
      Support     & \tfgcheckmark & \tfgcheckmark & --- & \tfgcheckmark & --- \\ \hline
    \end{tabular}}
  \caption{A table with its content rotated 90° in place (\texttt{\textbackslash rotatebox})}
  \label{tab:sideways}
\end{table}
